## What to Check Before Buying a Used Car in Ghana

### 1. Chassis Number (VIN)
The chassis number is stamped on the car's frame. Verify it matches the documents. Mismatched chassis numbers = stolen car.

### 2. Engine Check
- Start the car cold — should start easily
- Check for smoke (blue = oil burning, white = coolant leak, black = rich mixture)
- Listen for knocking sounds

### 3. Body & Frame
- Look for rust under the car and in the wheel arches
- Check for uneven panel gaps (sign of accident repair)
- Inspect all doors, bonnet, and boot for smooth operation

### 4. Transmission
- Automatic: Should shift smoothly through all gears
- Manual: Check clutch engagement — should not slip or shudder

### 5. Tyres
- All 4 tyres should be same brand and size
- Check tread depth — minimum 1.6mm legally required
- Look for uneven wear (sign of alignment issues)

### 6. Electrical Systems
- Test all lights (headlights, indicators, reversing lights)
- Check AC — should cool to very cold within 2 minutes
- Test all windows, mirrors, and central locking

### 7. Documents to Check
- ✅ Road Worthy Certificate (must be valid)
- ✅ Insurance Certificate
- ✅ Vehicle Registration (must match chassis number)
- ✅ Customs clearance documents (for imported cars)

## How to Avoid Car Buying Scams in Ghana

🚨 **Common scams to watch out for:**

1. **Cloned chassis numbers** — stolen cars with forged documents. Always verify with DVLA.
2. **Hidden flood damage** — cars that look fine but have water damage internally. Check under carpets for rust.
3. **Rolled-back odometer** — mileage manipulated to look lower. Check service history.
4. **"Quick sale" pressure** — scammers create urgency. Take your time — a good car will wait.
5. **Fake sellers** — especially online. Always meet the actual seller and see the physical car.

**Golden rule:** If it seems too cheap, it's too risky.
